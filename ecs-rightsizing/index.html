Method

Metrics: AWS/ECS namespace (CPUUtilization, MemoryUtilization) at 1-hour granularity over the 30 days ending 2026-06-09. For each service we compute average, median, p95, and p99 of hourly averages, plus the 30-day maximum of per-minute peaks. Hour-of-day patterns group hourly samples by UTC hour. Workload characterization comes from CloudWatch Logs samples and per-hour log volume.

"All-time max" is the worst single minute in the window. "p95/p99" are the sustained-load ceilings. Always-on services are costed at desired-count × 730 h; scale-to-zero workers (ml-worker, stats-worker) are costed on their measured active hours.

How conservative is this?

  • Every proposal is a valid Fargate CPU/memory pair — no infeasible "1 vCPU + 1 GB" shapes.
  • CPU is only halved where the all-time-max (or all but a lone deploy-minute) fits the smaller vCPU.
  • CPU-bound services (pro-cron, pro, dev-worker) keep their vCPU, which floors their memory — so they hold.
  • support's memory anomaly from the May report has cleared; it now holds on recurring CPU spikes instead.
  • Savings exclude staging clusters and the always-on data plane (RDS, queues, ALBs).
