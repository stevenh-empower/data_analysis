Method

Metrics: AWS/ECS namespace (CPUUtilization, MemoryUtilization) at 1-hour granularity over the 30 days ending 2026-05-15. For each service we compute average, median, p95, and p99 of hourly averages, plus the 30-day maximum of per-minute peaks. Hour-of-day patterns come from grouping hourly samples by UTC hour. Workload characterization comes from CloudWatch Logs samples and per-hour log volume.

"All-time max" is the worst single minute in the 30-day window — the worst case to protect against. "p95" is the 95th percentile of hourly averages — the sustained-load ceiling.

How conservative is this?

  • Allocations are sized against the all-time 30-day max, not the average.
  • Bursty CPU services keep their full vCPU allocation. Memory only drops where the ceiling has been provably stable.
  • One service (support-service) is held back despite trivial averages, because a real 8-hour memory event on Apr 27–28 hasn't been explained.
  • Savings exclude staging clusters and the always-on data plane (RDS, queues, ALBs).
